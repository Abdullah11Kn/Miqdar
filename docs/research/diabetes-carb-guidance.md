# Carbohydrate starting points for adults with diabetes

What recognised diabetes bodies say about carbs per meal and per snack, total versus net carbs, carb portions,
Ramadan meals, and disclaimers. Written for a planned Miqdar feature: a suggested starting carb number per meal
and snack, with a short explanation, which the user can accept or ignore until they see a doctor.

Checked on 7 November 2026. Each claim names its source. "Unconfirmed" means it could not be traced to the
owning body's own page or document.

## Answer

| Item | Suggested default for the app | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Main meal | 45 g carbs for women, 60 g for men. Show it as part of a 45 to 60 g range. | Medium that this is the usual taught starting point. Low that any current guideline endorses a number. |
| Snack | 15 g carbs (range 15 to 30 g). Snacks are optional. | Medium |
| Total or net | Count **total** carbs. Do not subtract fibre or sugar alcohols. | Medium to high |
| Carb portion | 1 portion = 15 g ("carb choice"). Show grams first, portions second. Note that the UK uses 10 g. | High for the definitions |
| Ramadan | Two main meals plus optional snacks. Split the day's carbs about 30 to 40% at suhoor, 40 to 50% at iftar, 10 to 20% in snacks. | Low: from summaries of the IDF-DAR plan, not the plan itself |
| Suhoor timing | Do not give advice. Say "follow your care team". | Low: "as late as possible" is unconfirmed |

Plain summary:

- No current guideline gives a fixed number. The ADA Standards of Care 2026, the ADA's public site, the CDC,
  Diabetes UK and the Saudi guideline all say amounts must be set for each person.
- The 45 to 60 g per meal figure comes from dietitian teaching material, and the CDC sample menu lands close to it.
  It is a reasonable starting point if the app says clearly that it is general and not personal.
- No body found says an app must not show a number. All of them say a dietitian or care team should set it.
- People on insulin or sulfonylureas, people with type 1 diabetes, pregnant people, children and people with
  kidney disease should be told to follow their care team rather than the app's number.

## Evidence

### Per meal and per snack

**ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes 2026, Section 5** (Diabetes Care 2026;49 Suppl 1:S89–S131,
doi 10.2337/dc26-S005, https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S89/163926). The journal page
refused automated access. The text was read from a proof copy downloaded from diabetesjournals.org and hosted at
https://education.weitzmaninstitute.org/sites/default/files/course/2025-12/Facilitating%20Positive%20Health%20Behaviors%20and%20Well-being%20to%20Improve%20Health%20Outcomes%20-%20Standards%20of%20Care%20in%20Diabetes-2026.pdf
- "There is no ideal percentage of calories from carbohydrate, protein, or fat for people with diabetes."
  Distribution "should be based on an individualized assessment of current eating patterns, preferences, and
  metabolic goals."
- 5.10: refer people with diabetes to a registered dietitian for individualised medical nutrition therapy (grade A).
- 5.15: "Consider reducing carbohydrate intake for some adults with diabetes to improve glycemia."
- 5.24: high-fibre carbohydrate sources, "at least 14 g fiber per 1,000 kcal".
- 5.28: people on fixed insulin doses should keep "consistent patterns of carbohydrate intake with respect to time
  and amount".
- Teaching tools named: "the plate method, carbohydrate choice, carbohydrate counting". The plate is one half
  non-starchy vegetables, one quarter protein, one quarter carbohydrate. Carbohydrate counting is "a more advanced
  skill".
- Very-low-carb plans are "not currently recommended" in pregnancy, lactation, children, kidney disease or
  disordered eating, and should be avoided with SGLT2 inhibitors.
- No grams per meal or per snack appear in the section.

**American Diabetes Association, public site** ("Carb counting and diabetes",
https://diabetes.org/food-nutrition/understanding-carbs/carb-counting-and-diabetes)
- "there's no magic number". Needs depend on body size, activity, appetite.
- One carb choice is "about 15 grams of carb".
- A dietitian or diabetes care and education specialist "can help you figure out what works best for you".
- The often-quoted "ADA says 45 to 60 g per meal" is not on this page. Unconfirmed as a current ADA position.

**CDC** ("Carb Counting", https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/healthy-eating/carb-counting-manage-blood-sugar.html,
dated May 2024)
- "There's no 'one size fits all' answer".
- "Try to eat about the same amount of carbs at each meal".
- "1 carb serving is about 15 grams of carbs."
- Sample menu, "about 1,800 calories, 200 grams of carbs": breakfast 65 g, lunch 59 g, dinner 57 g, snack 19 g.
  This is an example, not a target.
- Refers people to diabetes self-management education "to create a healthy meal plan just for you".

**Diabetes Care and Education Dietetic Practice Group** (American Dietetic Association, now the Academy of
Nutrition and Dietetics), handout "Ready, Set, Start Counting", 2003, updated 2007,
https://dpg-storage.s3.amazonaws.com/dce/resources/ReadySetStart_final_revised.pdf
- "Most women need about 3 to 4 carbohydrate choices (45 to 60 grams) at each meal".
- "Most men need about 4 to 5 carbohydrate choices (60 to 75 grams) at each meal".
- "If you eat snacks, choose 1 to 2 carbohydrate choices (15 to 30 grams)".
- The number "depends on your body size, activity level and weight management goals".
- "1 carbohydrate choice = 15 grams of carbohydrate".
- This is the oldest source here and its reproduction permission ended in 2010. It is the only primary source
  found for the 45 to 60 g figure.

**ADCES** (Association of Diabetes Care & Education Specialists). Its healthy eating handout
(https://www.adces.org/docs/default-source/handouts/adces7/handout_pwd_adces7_healthyeating.pdf) refused automated
access. No ADCES per-meal number was confirmed.

**Diabetes UK** ("Carbohydrates and diabetes",
https://www.diabetes.org.uk/living-with-diabetes/eating/carbohydrates-and-diabetes)
- No fixed amount: "The actual amount that you need to eat will depend on your age, activity levels and the
  health goals". "not everyone with diabetes needs to restrict their carb intake."
- People on fixed insulin doses should keep carb amounts consistent.
- Adults should aim for "30g of fibre per day".
- Advises talking to the healthcare team about medicines that can cause hypos if cutting carbs.

**NHS** ("Counting carbohydrates", type 1 diabetes,
https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/type-1-diabetes/learning-to-count-carbohydrates/counting-carbohydrates/, reviewed
31 October 2024). Explains why counting helps and points to courses such as DAFNE. Gives no gram figures and no
portion size.

**Saudi Diabetes Clinical Practice Guidelines** (Saudi National Diabetes Center and Saudi Health Council, first
edition 2021, https://www.moh.gov.sa/Ministry/MediaCenter/Publications/Documents/Saudi-Diabetes-Clinical-Practice-Guidelines.pdf,
pages 35 to 37)
- "There is no evidence indicating an ideal dietary distribution of calories among carbohydrates, fats, and
  proteins for people with DM; therefore, meal plans should be individualized".
- Fixed insulin: "a consistent carbohydrate intake pattern with respect to time and amount is recommended".
- "Develop a nutrition therapy plan for each patient ... to be provided by a registered nutritionist".
- Strongly advises teaching carbohydrate counting around diagnosis for type 1.
- Gives no grams per meal. Its hypo section uses 15 g of glucose, then a 15 to 20 g complex carbohydrate snack.

**Saudi Ministry of Health, Arabic consumer material.** A secondary Arabic site attributes 50 to 60% of daily
energy from carbs and 15 g portions to the Ministry. Unconfirmed: no Ministry page with these figures was found.

### Total versus net carbs

- ADA public site: total carbohydrate "includes all carbs: sugar, starch and fiber". The page talks only about
  total carbs and does not mention net carbs or sugar alcohols.
- Dietetic handout above: use "Total Carbohydrate"; sugars are already inside it.
- CDC: "Sugars and starches raise your blood sugar, but fiber doesn't." It does not say to subtract fibre.
- Diabetes UK labels page (https://www.diabetes.org.uk/guide-to-diabetes/enjoy-food/food-shopping-for-diabetes/understanding-food-labels):
  check "the total carbohydrate, which includes carbohydrates from starchy food as well as sugars". No mention of
  net carbs or polyols.
- The rule "subtract half the fibre when over 5 g, and half the sugar alcohols" is widely credited to the ADA, but
  only secondary sources were found and they disagree on the thresholds (5 g or 10 g). Unconfirmed. "Net carbs" is
  not defined by any body checked here.
- **Labels mean different things in different places.** This matters for food weighed in the Gulf:
  - US: total carbohydrate is calculated by difference, so it contains fibre and sugar alcohols (21 CFR 101.9(c)(6),
    https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/101.9).
  - EU: "carbohydrate" means "any carbohydrate which is metabolised by humans, and includes polyols". Fibre is
    listed separately, so it is not in the carbohydrate figure (Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011, Annex I,
    https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32011R1169).
  - Saudi and Gulf (GSO) labels: not checked. Unconfirmed which convention they follow.

  So "total carbs" on a US label is higher than "carbohydrate" on an EU label for the same food. The app should
  say which one its number means. Counting digestible carbs including sugar alcohols, without subtracting anything
  else, matches both the EU definition and the "count total" advice closely enough for a starting point.

### How carb counting is taught

- US and ADA teaching: 1 carb choice or serving = 15 g (ADA site, CDC, dietetic handout above).
- ADA Standards 2026 places the plate method first for basic planning and calls carb counting "a more advanced
  skill". It notes newer tools such as smartphone apps, CGM and automated insulin delivery "may reduce the need for
  precise carbohydrate counting".
- UK teaching: 1 carbohydrate portion (CP) = 10 g. Example from an NHS trust: 200 g cooked rice at 30 g per 100 g
  is 60 g, "That's 6 CPs" (Whittington Health NHS Trust, https://www.whittington.nhs.uk/default.asp?c=9675). Other
  NHS trust leaflets that use 10 g were seen only in search results.
- Saudi guideline: uses 15 g in its hypo treatment. No Saudi body defining a portion size was found.

### Ramadan

**IDF and DAR International Alliance, "Diabetes and Ramadan: Practical guidelines 2021"** (Hassanein et al.,
Diabetes Research and Clinical Practice 2022;185:109185, doi 10.1016/j.diabres.2021.109185; read from the copy at
https://medbox.org/dl/665312384f627c07d568cef3)
- Suhoor is "the meal eaten before dawn", iftar "the meal served at the end of day".
- "Eating large quantities at and after Iftar can cause further rises in blood glucose levels."
- "Dietary recommendations to people with diabetes need to be individualised".
- The Ramadan Nutrition Plan (RNP) is a tool for health professionals: "This tool should be used by HCPs when
  providing nutrition related guidance." Its use is graded D, "largely based on expert opinion".
- "An individualised nutrition plan should be made prior to Ramadan".
- Pre-Ramadan assessment "ideally, 6–8 weeks before the start of Ramadan".
- This journal summary gives no percentages. It sends readers to the full guideline on idf.org and daralliance.org.
  The idf.org page blocked automated access and daralliance.org did not resolve, so the full RNP chapter was not
  read.

**RNP figures, from secondary sources only (unconfirmed against the IDF-DAR full text):**
- Mehar S, Journal of Diabetes Nursing 2024;28:JDN318 (https://diabetesonthenet.com/wp-content/uploads/JDN_28-1_JDN318.pdf),
  Box 1, "The 10 principles of the Ramadan Nutrition Plan (IDF-DAR, 2021)": "Divide daily calories between Suhoor
  and Iftar"; "45–50% carbohydrate", "20–30% protein", "<35% fat"; low-GI, high-fibre foods; avoid sugary desserts;
  water or unsweetened drinks between sunset and sunrise.
- Ahmed WN et al., J Family Med Prim Care 2020;9:3797–3806 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7586529/),
  Table 3, citing the 2017 IDF-DAR guideline: "Suhoor (30-40%) + Iftar (40-50%) + Snacks (10-20%) once or twice
  between meals if necessary"; fibre 20 to 35 g a day; "Avoid large calorie dense meal".
- Neither source gives grams of carbs per meal.

**Suhoor timing.** "Take suhoor as late as possible" appears in clinical summaries of IDF-DAR advice, but not in the
2021 journal text above. Unconfirmed. Search results also described a randomised trial in type 1 diabetes where
early and late suhoor had similar rates of fasts broken by hypoglycaemia. That trial was not read.

**ADA Standards 2026, religious fasting** (5.32, 5.33): use the IDF-DAR risk score, give fasting-focused education,
and adjust treatment. The meal that breaks the fast "is rich in carbohydrates"; advice should emphasise "higher
intake of fiber and replacing added sugars with complex carbohydrates".

**Diabetes UK with the British Islamic Medical Association**, "Ramadan and diabetes", reviewed December 2023
(https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/1922/6045/files/Ramadan_and_Diabetes_-_English_LR.pdf, from the Diabetes UK
shop)
- At suhoor, "choose high fibre, slow-release carbohydrates such as: wholegrain bread, oats and wholegrains,
  brown or wild rice".
- At iftar, "keep fried, oily and sugary foods to a minimum".
- "If you get symptoms of a hypo, you must break your fast immediately". No grams.

**Saudi guideline 2021, page 89 onward.** Pre-Ramadan education should cover "food" among other topics. It uses the
2017 IDF-DAR risk categories. One sentence reads "people with T1DM and T2DM should be advised not to fast because of
high risk of complications"; read in context it seems to mean the high-risk groups, but the wording is broad.

**Saudi Ministry of Health news pages**
- 2019 (https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/Ministry/MediaCenter/News/Pages/news-2019-05-05-003.aspx): "dividing
  breakfast into several moderate meals"; "reduce the intake of foods high in sugar and fats".
- 2020 (https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/ministry/mediacenter/news/pages/news-2020-05-07-003.aspx): eat complex carbs
  "such as: brown bread and brown rice". Aimed at everyone fasting.
- 2014, an endocrinologist on the Ministry's site
  (https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/Ministry/MediaCenter/News/Pages/News-2014-07-05-001.aspx): "two meals interspersed by
  a smaller meal"; avoid dates, juices, fried food and desserts. Old advice.
- None give grams.

### Should an app give a number? Disclaimers

- No diabetes body checked here says an app or a non-clinician must not show a carb number.
- Every body says the amount should be individual and set with a dietitian, educator or care team
  (ADA Standards 5.10; ADA site; CDC; Diabetes UK; Saudi guideline; IDF-DAR). IDF-DAR says its Ramadan tool is for
  health professionals.
- ADA Standards 2026 says apps, CGM and automated insulin delivery "may reduce the need for precise carbohydrate
  counting". None of the bodies endorse a number shown by an app.
- Regulation was not researched in depth. US FDA updated its general wellness and clinical decision support
  guidance in January 2026; summaries say a disease-related claim can make software a medical device. Saudi SFDA
  rules were not checked. Unconfirmed for this app.

Suggested wording, based on what the bodies say:

> This is a general starting point, not a plan made for you. Diabetes groups such as the ADA and Diabetes UK say
> the right amount differs from person to person. Ask your doctor or dietitian for your own number. If you take
> insulin or tablets that can cause low blood sugar, keep following your care team's plan.

Show the suggestion only to adults who did not mark pregnancy, and add a stronger "talk to your care team first"
for type 1 diabetes, insulin or sulfonylurea use, kidney disease, and Ramadan fasting.

## Open questions

1. Should the default be one number (45 g women, 60 g men) or a range (45 to 60 g)? The only primary source for
   these numbers is a 2007 dietitian handout.
2. The app already calculates a daily carb goal from the body profile. Should meal numbers come from that goal
   instead (for example, a quarter of it per meal), so the two never disagree?
3. Which carb definition do Miqdar's food values and Gemini's estimates use: US total (with fibre) or EU style
   (without)? The suggestion and the meal count must use the same one.
4. Saudi and Gulf (GSO) label rules for carbohydrate and fibre were not checked.
5. The full IDF-DAR 2021 Ramadan Nutrition Plan chapter was not read. Its percentages and its suhoor timing advice
   should be checked against it before the Ramadan split goes into the app.
6. ADCES material and any Saudi Ministry or Saudi Diabetes and Endocrine Association page with per-meal numbers
   were not reached.
7. Whether showing a carb number to people with diabetes changes the app's regulatory status in Saudi Arabia.
