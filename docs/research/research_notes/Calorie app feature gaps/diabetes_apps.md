# Diabetes-focused food and carbohydrate tracking apps: features, evidence, safety lines and gaps for Miqdar

Research date: 2026-11-14. Miqdar's current diabetes mode, as briefed: a diabetes question in the profile; carbs shown first in grams and in 15 g portions; a per-meal carb number (45 or 60 g suggested, or the doctor's own); red warnings before eating when a meal passes free-sugar, calorie or carb limits; a free-sugar limit of 5% of calories (WHO), chosen by the user, with a notice that Miqdar takes no medical responsibility; a doctor's PDF report; Ramadan mode. It has no glucose readings, no insulin logging and no CGM link.

## What do the leading diabetes and carb apps offer?

### Takeaway
The market has split into two groups. One is the logbook apps (mySugr, Gluroo, Undermyfork, Sugarmate): they put glucose, insulin, carbs and meals on one timeline and often add a bolus calculator or CGM-linked insights. The other is the CGM-maker apps (Dexcom G7 and Stelo, Abbott Lingo), which since 2025 have added AI photo meal logging so the user can see how a meal changed their glucose. No app found pairs a measured weight with the carb estimate, and that is Miqdar's distinct advantage. The common feature Miqdar lacks is a link between meal and glucose: glucose or CGM data, usually read through Apple Health, shown against each meal.

### Cited Findings
**mySugr (Roche)**
- The free tier logs meals, medication, insulin therapy and carbohydrate intake. Pro adds meal photos, which the company says help improve carb counting. It also adds PDF and Excel reports, a basal rate for pump users, blood sugar reminders and challenges. Basic includes an estimated HbA1c, CSV-only reports, tags and backup. — [mySugr app page](https://mysugr.com/app); [ADCES DANAtech listing](https://www.adces.org/education/danatech/apps-dtx/find-apps-tools/product-detail/mysugr)
- The bolus calculator is a Pro feature, offered only in some countries, for users over 18, and described as a risk class IIb medical device. The Australian price was $4.49 a month or $42.99 a year. Pro can be unlocked for free with some Accu-Chek meters, which also upload readings automatically. — [Accu-Chek Australia, mySugr](https://www.accu-chek.com.au/mysugr-app); [Accu-Chek UK, mySugr](https://accu-chek.co.uk/mysugr-app)
- mySugr is listed in the Saudi App Store. Which features are available in Saudi Arabia, including the bolus calculator, was not confirmed. — [App Store SA](https://apps.apple.com/sa/app/id516509211)

**Dexcom Stelo and G7 (CGM-maker apps)**
- In July 2025 Dexcom launched AI "Smart Food Logging" for Stelo, its over-the-counter CGM; G7 already had it. The user photographs a meal and the AI names the ingredients and fills in the meal description. The same release added a "Glucose Impact" view showing how logged events affect glucose, AI-written weekly insights and an Oura integration. — [Dexcom press release, BusinessWire, 2025-07-28](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250728353105/en/Dexcom-Launches-Revolutionary-AI-Powered-Meal-Logging-Feature-Across-Glucose-Biosensing-Portfolio/)
- Stelo's help page says the AI description can be edited and the photo is optional. — [Stelo FAQ](https://www.stelo.com/en-us/faqs/using-stelo/how-do-i-log-photo-of-meal-activity-in-stelo-app)
- A February 2026 Stelo update announced a food database of more than a million items. It gives calories, carbs, protein, fat and fiber for each meal, logged by text search, barcode or photo, and redesigned the Daily Insights. — [Nasdaq / Dexcom press release](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/stelo-adds-enhanced-smart-meal-logging-features-dexcom-continues-transform-personal)
- Wareable says the redesign predicts a meal's glucose curve from a photo. No Dexcom source was found that confirms this, so it is unverified. — [Wareable](https://www.wareable.com/health-and-wellbeing/dexcom-stelo-cgm-pediatric-fda-clearance-new-ai-metabolic-meal-logging)

**Abbott Lingo (over-the-counter CGM, wellness)**
- It logs food and activity "for even deeper insights", syncs workouts from Apple Health, and lets the user log a meal by photo: Lingo names the foods and fills in the details. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1670445335); [Lingo blog: photo-logging meals](https://www.hellolingo.com/blog/how-to-photo-log-meals)
- One reviewer called the insights eye-opening but the food logging "really tedious". — [Wareable review](https://www.wareable.com/health-and-wellbeing/abbott-launches-its-lingo-biowearable-in-the-us-starts-at-49)

**Undermyfork**
- A photo food diary. It combines CGM or meter glucose with each meal photo, works out the time in range in the two hours after the meal, and colors each meal green, amber or red. On iPhone it reads glucose through Apple Health (Dexcom, Libre through add-ons, some meters). On Android it reads Dexcom only. — [diaTribe](https://diatribe.org/diabetes-technology/diabetes-food-logging-app-undermyfork-launches-android-users-and-healthcare)
- It also takes manual glucose and insulin entries. It is free for individuals, and Undermyfork Care shares the meal data with the care team and family. — [ADCES DANAtech listing](https://www.adces.org/education/danatech/apps-dtx/find-apps-tools/product-detail/under-my-fork)

**Gluroo**
- A free iOS and Android logger. A photo or a typed description gives carbs, calories, fat, protein and fiber. Several caregivers can follow glucose and log meals and doses, shared in real time. It works with Dexcom, Libre and Omnipod 5, and has an AI chatbot for finding patterns in meal responses. — [Gluroo home](https://gluroo.com/); [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gluroo-diabetes-logger/id1560748689)
- The App Store listing warns that dosing decisions should not be based on it. A release note says photo analysis became twice as fast or more. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gluroo-diabetes-logger/id1560748689); [Gluroo release notes](https://gluroo.com/blog/page/3/)
- No independent test of how accurate its photo estimates are was found.

**Sugarmate (Tandem)**
- Bought by Tandem Diabetes Care in 2020. It offers a CGM companion feed, a food database, a diary, exercise detection, and an automatic phone call to a chosen number when readings are low during sleep. It is a Dexcom data partner. — [Tandem, Sugarmate](https://www.tandemdiabetes.com/products/software-apps/sugarmate); [Beyond Type 1](https://beyondtype1.org/using-sugarmate/); [Dexcom integrations](https://www.dexcom.com/en-gb/integrations-and-connectivity/digital-health-apps/sugarmate)

**General carb trackers: Carb Manager, Cronometer, MyFitnessPal**
- Carb Manager's help pages describe diabetes carb options (UCSF or ADA definitions), blood glucose and ketone logging, and Apple Health sync. A forum reply says Apple Health receives only total carbs, not the diabetes carb figure. — [Carb Manager help, tracking diabetes](https://help.carbmanager.com/docs/tracking-diabetes); [Carb Manager forum](https://forums.carbmanager.com/t/h7h1tcf/export-diabetes-carbs-to-apple-health)
- Cronometer and MyFitnessPal: the only sources found were comparison posts by a competing app (Nutrola). They say Cronometer shows net carbs with fiber, takes blood glucose as a custom entry and reads CGM data through Apple Health, and that MyFitnessPal's carb data is crowdsourced and has little glucose support. This is unverified marketing. — [Nutrola comparison, 2026](https://nutrola.app/en/blog/myfitnesspal-vs-cronometer-for-diabetes-2026)

### Inferences
- Features common across these apps that Miqdar lacks, roughly from most to least common:
  1. Glucose shown beside each meal: before and after values, the two-hour response, or Undermyfork's time-in-range color. This is the core of Undermyfork, Stelo, Lingo and Gluroo.
  2. Reading CGM and meter data through Apple Health or Health Connect, rather than through each maker's own connection. Read-only access is technically within reach for a Flutter app.
  3. Manual logging of glucose and insulin (mySugr, Undermyfork, Gluroo). Logging is low risk; dose advice is not (see the regulation section).
  4. An estimated HbA1c from logged glucose (mySugr Basic).
  5. Sharing with family or the care team (Gluroo, Undermyfork Care). Miqdar's doctor PDF covers part of this.
  6. Low-glucose alerts and calls (Sugarmate). These need live CGM data and are a medical-device area; not suitable for Miqdar.
  7. Bolus calculators (mySugr Pro). These are regulated medical devices; not suitable for Miqdar.
- No app found uses a measured weight. All of the photo AIs (Dexcom, Lingo, Gluroo) estimate the portion from the image. Miqdar's scale removes the largest error source shown in the evidence below, which is a defensible "why Miqdar" point for diabetes users.
- Miqdar's warnings before eating are unusual. The CGM apps show the effect after eating. Miqdar can say "this plate is 80 g of carbs, above your 60 g" before the first bite, and no surveyed app was found doing that from a weighed plate.

### Gaps
- Glucose Buddy, One Drop, the Libre companion apps (LibreLink, Libre 3) and the diabetes features of MyFitnessPal were not researched in depth, for lack of tool budget. One Drop's current status is unclear: no evidence was found that it shut down, and the newest listing seen was updated on 2024-05-30 ([AppWisp](https://appwisp.com/app/android/today.onedrop.android)).
- Which of these apps' features are available in Saudi Arabia, and in Arabic, was not checked.
- Glycemic index and glycemic load: none of the dedicated apps surveyed was confirmed to show them.

## What do guidelines and studies say helps, including carb counting accuracy, glycemic load, post-meal glucose and photo-based carb estimates?

### Takeaway
How well a person counts carbs is linked to better glucose control, but people count carbs poorly, and trials of carb counting alone show modest or mixed benefit. Photo AI makes the portion problem worse: general AI models are inconsistent, often wrong about the dish, and badly wrong on medium and large portions. A measured weight removes much of that error. Glycemic index and load have only modest, grade-B support. The ADA's 2026 Standards support digital self-management education.

### Cited Findings
**Carb counting**
- A 2024 review in *Nutrients* says patients still find it hard to estimate a meal's carbs, that the errors carry into the insulin dose, and that tools for assessing counting skill are limited. It calls for individual education. — [Assessing Carbohydrate Counting Accuracy, Nutrients 2024, PMC11279647](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11279647/)
- Bell et al.'s 2014 systematic review found "some evidence" for carb counting over other advice in adults with type 1 diabetes. Conference coverage of the same work reports no significant effect on glucose control overall, a trend toward fewer low-glucose events, and better counting accuracy linked with lower HbA1c. The participant numbers disagree between sources. — [PubMed 24622717](https://www.pubmed.gov/24622717/); [The Hospitalist](https://blogs.the-hospitalist.org/content/counting-carbs-comes-short-type-1-diabetes)

**Photo and AI carb estimates**
- Diabettech consistency study, a preprint posted 2026-04-15 and submitted to *Diabetologia*. Thirteen meal photos were each sent more than 500 times to GPT-5.4, Claude Sonnet 4.6, Gemini 2.5 Pro and Gemini 3.1 Pro, for 26,904 queries in all. — [Diabettech](https://www.diabettech.com/i-asked-ai-to-count-my-carbs-27000-times)
  - Every model gave different carb answers for the same photo. The median coefficient of variation was 2.4% for Claude, 8.4% for GPT-5.4, 10.3% for Gemini 3.1 Pro and 11.0% for Gemini 2.5 Pro.
  - Gemini 2.5 Pro's estimates for one paella photo ranged from 55 g to 484 g, a gap equal to 42.9 units of insulin at a 1:10 ratio.
  - Every model overestimated on average. Dish identification errors appeared in 8 of 13 images.
  - The models' own confidence scores did not track accuracy (Claude r = −0.01).
  - The author's conclusions: no model is safe for unsupervised insulin dosing; asking several times and checking the spread is the only useful uncertainty signal; users should check the identified food before trusting the number. He cites a UK Diabetes Technology Network statement that generic AI models must not be used as autonomous insulin dosing calculators.
- *Nutrients* 2025 (ChatGPT-4 with meal photos): weight agreement was good for small meals but poor for medium and large ones, and nutrient estimates were poor. — [Nutrients 17:607](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/nutrients/nutrients-17-00607/article_deploy/nutrients-17-00607.pdf)
- A ChatGPT-4o study in adolescents with type 1 diabetes concluded that they should use it for carb estimates "with caution". The figures could not be seen, and the only link found was a proxy. — [SAGE, J Diabetes Sci Technol, doi 10.1177/19322968261424270](https://https-sage-cnpereading-com-443.webvpn1.xju.edu.cn/doi/10.1177/19322968261424270)
- A GPT-4o one-shot study in type 2 diabetes found fiber estimates accurate but other nutrients inaccurate. Gemini 2.5 Flash on the Nutrition5k benchmark had a carb error of 56.6%, falling to 39.5% when given a predicted weight (78 cases). Both are summarized from search results; the full papers were not opened. — [ODU Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.odu.edu/computerscience_fac_pubs/417); [Preprints.org 202605.0623](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202605.0623)

**Glycemic index and load**
- The ADA's 2013 nutrition recommendations (grade B) say that swapping high-glycemic-load foods for low ones "may modestly improve glycemic control", and that evidence on the ideal carb amount is inconclusive. Trials of low versus high glycemic index diets show mixed effects on HbA1c. — [Franz et al., JADA 2010](https://andeal.org/files/Docs/JADA_2010_110_12_Franz.pdf); [Today's Dietitian](https://www.todaysdietitian.com/blood-sugar-control-is-it-just-about-carb-quality/)
- Dietitians warn that glycemic load "can be misleading and complicated for the average patient". — [Today's Dietitian](https://www.todaysdietitian.com/blood-sugar-control-is-it-just-about-carb-quality/)

**ADA Standards of Care 2026** (released 2025-12-08)
- Recommendation 5.6 allows diabetes self-management education and support to be delivered by telehealth or digital tools, backed by a 2023 meta-analysis showing moderate evidence for apps, coaching and SMS. The Standards also cover low-carbohydrate and Mediterranean eating patterns. — [ACP Diabetes Guide summary](https://diabetesguide.acponline.org/archives/2025/12/12/1.htm); [Medical Economics](https://www.medicaleconomics.com/view/what-s-new-in-the-american-diabetes-association-s-2026-diabetes-care-standards); [Standards 2026, PMC12690188](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690188)

**What makes people keep using an app**
- In a JMIR Diabetes qualitative study of 25 adults with type 2 diabetes testing four apps, personal health was the main reason to use one, and the effort of learning an app was "quite high". — [Bults et al., JMIR Diabetes 2023](https://doaj.org/article/2570a30e24cf4168b526a2905e310c17)

### Inferences
- Miqdar's design already addresses the largest error source in the evidence. Portion size is where photo AI fails most, on medium and large plates and mixed dishes like paella, kabsa or jareesh, and Miqdar measures the grams instead. The remaining error comes from naming the dish and from its carbs per 100 g.
- The Diabettech result applies to Miqdar directly, since it uses Gemini, the model family that varied most there. Three changes follow:
  1. Show the identified food prominently before the carb number. Miqdar already shows the name and allows Change Food.
  2. Do not present a confidence percentage as a safety signal; the study found confidence scores meaningless. Abdullah decided the confidence display stays until he removes it himself, so this is evidence to bring to him, not a change to make alone.
  3. Optionally ask more than once, or flag a high-variance result. The free Gemini quota makes repeated queries costly.
- In Gulf food, glycemic index or a "carb quality" hint would add little measured benefit (modest, grade B) and some confusion. It could be a low-priority, plain-language hint, such as "white rice dish: fast-acting carbs", rather than a GI number.
- The strongest gap the evidence supports is showing post-meal glucose against a weighed meal: it is how users learn their own response. Even manual entry of a reading taken two hours after eating would let the doctor's report pair carbs with glucose.

### Gaps
- The exact 2026 ADA text on carb counting and on choosing or recommending apps (Sections 5 and 7) was not read in full, only secondary summaries.
- No IDF statement on apps was found.
- No study was found on how accurate AI carb estimates are when the weight is measured rather than estimated. The Gemini 2.5 Flash benchmark improved only with a *predicted* weight. This is the study Miqdar itself could run as its accuracy test.
- No study of Gulf or Saudi foods was found.

## What regulatory and safety lines must a student project not cross (FDA, SFDA)?

### Takeaway
Logging food, calculating nutrients, coaching on healthy eating and sharing logs with a doctor are outside regulation or low risk under both FDA and SFDA. Recommending or adjusting insulin doses, and analyzing glucose readings to raise out-of-range alerts, are medical device functions. The Saudi guidance (MDS-G27, August 2025) is stricter in one place that matters for Miqdar: it lists an app that tracks episodes and "generates reports for their healthcare providers" as an example of a medical device. If Miqdar is treated as a general wellness product, the label must say in Arabic and English that it is not for medical purposes.

### Cited Findings
**FDA**
- The FDA's *Policy for Device Software Functions and Mobile Medical Applications*, current as of 2022-09-28, says FDA oversight applies to device software functions "whose functionality could pose a risk to a patient's safety". — [FDA guidance page](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/policy-device-software-functions-and-mobile-medical-applications)
- Outside FDA oversight, from the guidance PDF ([FDA PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/80958/download)):
  - Software that helps people "log, record, track, evaluate, or make decisions or behavioral suggestions related to … general fitness, health or wellness". This includes software that will "Provide dietary logs, calorie counters, or make dietary suggestions" and "meal planners and recipes".
  - Where it sits: Appendix A ("not medical devices"), as far as the text extract can be read.
- Low risk, where FDA uses enforcement discretion (Appendix B, [FDA PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/80958/download)):
  - Software that coaches patients with "diabetes, or obesity" and promotes "getting optimal nutrition … or adhering to pre-determined medication dosing schedules by simple prompting".
  - "Simple tools for patients with specific conditions or chronic disease (e.g., … diabetes …) to record their events or measurements (e.g., … drug intake times, diet …) and share this information with their health care professional".
  - Software that lets "a user to record … blood glucose … to eventually share with a heath care professional" (quoted exactly; "heath" is the PDF's own spelling).
- The focus of FDA oversight ([FDA PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/80958/download)):
  - Software that uses "patient-specific parameters and calculate[s] dosage or create[s] a dosage plan".
  - A "software function that analyzes readings from a blood glucose monitor and alerts the user of readings outside a predetermined range".
  - Apps that control insulin delivery on a pump.
  - Software that uses a phone attachment to measure blood glucose.
- Insulin dose apps have gone through FDA review: Lilly Go Dose, Sanofi My Dose Coach and Voluntis Insulia around 2017, and later a CGM-informed bolus calculator from Welldoc. — [MobiHealthNews, Sanofi](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/sanofi-gets-fda-clearance-insulin-dose-calculator-app); [MobiHealthNews, Lilly](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/eli-lilly-gets-fda-clearance-insulin-dose-calculator-app); [Pharmacy Times](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/insulin-dosing-apps-are-coming-and-patients-will-use-them)
- In the EU, mySugr's bolus calculator is a class IIb medical device. — [Accu-Chek UK](https://accu-chek.co.uk/mysugr-app)

**SFDA (Saudi Arabia)**
- MDS-G27 *Guidance on Digital Health Products*, version 1.0, dated 11/8/2025 in the PDF (August 2025 by its file path). It draws the line between regulated medical devices and general wellness products. A product that qualifies as a medical device needs a Medical Devices Marketing Authorization (MDMA, under MDS-REQ 1). — [SFDA MDS-G27 PDF](https://www.sfda.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2025-08/MDS-G027.pdf)
- Its examples of apps that are medical devices: "An app that helps epilepsy patients track seizure episodes and generates reports for their healthcare providers", and an app using sensors "to monitor blood glucose levels and recommend insulin dosage adjustments for diabetic patients". — [SFDA MDS-G27, §1.2.2](https://www.sfda.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2025-08/MDS-G027.pdf)
- Its examples of apps that are not medical devices: "An app that sends reminders for medication intake without managing dosage or providing drug-specific recommendations", and "An app that allows users to log meals and calculate nutritional content for general wellness purposes". — [SFDA MDS-G27, §1.2.2](https://www.sfda.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2025-08/MDS-G027.pdf)
- General wellness (§2):
  - A product can mention a chronic disease such as type 2 diabetes and stay general wellness, as long as its claim "is unrelated to the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, prevention, or treatment" and is about living well with the disease.
  - If a wellness product is "recommended, used, or prescribed" by medical professionals, it is subject to SFDA assessment, and "any identified off-label medical use … shall be promptly reported to the SFDA".
  - Labeling (§2.1): the manufacturer "shall clearly state in the labeling (in both Arabic and English) that the device is not intended for medical purposes".
  - Source: [SFDA MDS-G27, §2 and §2.1](https://www.sfda.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2025-08/MDS-G027.pdf)
- SFDA follows the IMDRF principles for software as a medical device, in MDS-G23 (version 2, 2024). — [SFDA MDS-G23 v2](https://www.sfda.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2026-11/MDS-G023%20%28V.2%29.pdf)

**Professional bodies**
- The UK Diabetes Technology Network says generic AI models must not be used as autonomous insulin dosing calculators. This is reported second-hand by Diabettech; the original was not opened. — [Diabettech](https://www.diabettech.com/i-asked-ai-to-count-my-carbs-27000-times)

### Inferences
- Lines Miqdar must not cross:
  1. Any insulin dose, insulin-to-carb ratio or correction suggestion.
  2. Analyzing glucose readings to raise out-of-range or low-glucose alerts.
  3. Predicting glucose from a meal as a treatment aid.
  4. Words like "treat", "prevent", "manage your diabetes" or "verified".
  Logging glucose or insulin manually, or reading them from Apple Health, and showing them beside meals, is on the safe side under FDA (Appendix B). Under SFDA it is safe as long as no dose advice follows.
- The red warnings are worth a check. They compare a meal with a carb, calorie or free-sugar limit, which is dietary advice, not glucose analysis, so they fit "make dietary suggestions" under FDA and "log meals and calculate nutritional content" under SFDA. Stricter wording would also be safer: for example "above your chosen carb number for this meal", not "unsafe for your diabetes".
- The doctor's PDF report is the closest feature to an SFDA medical device example, the epilepsy app that "generates reports for their healthcare providers". FDA puts sharing logs with a doctor under enforcement discretion. The report does no medical interpretation and no AI analysis, which is the best defense. To keep it on the wellness side, it should be presented as a copy of the user's own food log, not a clinical assessment. This is a judgment from the guidance text, not a ruling.
- Miqdar should state, in Arabic and English, that it is not intended for medical purposes, as MDS-G27 §2.1 requires. The existing no-medical-responsibility notice is part of this; it should be checked against that wording and shown in both languages.
- As a student project that is not sold, Miqdar is not going to market, so MDMA does not apply now. The design should still stay on the wellness side, so that a later launch in Saudi Arabia does not need SFDA authorization.

### Gaps
- No SFDA risk class was found for bolus calculators, and MDS-G008's classification tables were not read.
- No source was found on how SFDA treats non-commercial or academic apps.
- Which FDA appendix holds each example is read from a rough text extract of the PDF; the appendix placement should be checked in the PDF before it is quoted formally.
- The UK DTN statement was not read in the original.

## What do users with diabetes complain about in these apps?

### Takeaway
The biggest complaint is that the app fails to connect to devices and the data is lost or out of reach, not the food features. Food logging is described as tedious. Effort to learn the app is high. Paywalls and limited regional availability of key features recur.

### Cited Findings
- Turner and Stawarz, JMIR Diabetes 2025: 21 diabetes apps with device connections and 602 user reviews. — [JMIR Diabetes 2025;10:e62926](https://diabetes.jmir.org/2025/1/e62926)
  - 54.5% of the reviews were negative, 31.1% positive and 14.6% neutral or mixed. 62.8% mentioned problems.
  - Most common problems: cannot connect to devices (25.1%), cannot store, manage or access data (22%), unreliable notifications and alerts (9.2%), problems after software updates (8.5%), hardware faults (6.4%).
  - Most discussed features: device connections (43%), tracking (32.2%), data logging (14.3%), notifications (11.6%).
- Lingo's food logging was called "really tedious" by a reviewer. — [Wareable](https://www.wareable.com/health-and-wellbeing/abbott-launches-its-lingo-biowearable-in-the-us-starts-at-49)
- Bults et al.: participants found the effort of learning an app "quite high". — [JMIR Diabetes 2023](https://doaj.org/article/2570a30e24cf4168b526a2905e310c17)
- Gluroo: a third-party review summary reports battery drain and limited features for pump users. It is a secondary aggregator. — [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.gluroo.app)
- Sugarmate: an April 2026 user review mentions problems with the app working. — [JustUseApp reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1111093108/sugarmate/reviews)
- Carb Manager: Apple Health receives only total carbs, not the diabetes carb figure, which annoys users who need it elsewhere. — [Carb Manager forum](https://forums.carbmanager.com/t/h7h1tcf/export-diabetes-carbs-to-apple-health)
- mySugr: key features are behind Pro ($4.49 a month) and the bolus calculator is limited by country. — [Accu-Chek Australia](https://www.accu-chek.com.au/mysugr-app)
- AI photo logging: the inconsistent answers and wrong dish names found by Diabettech are a safety and trust complaint from the diabetes tech community. — [Diabettech](https://www.diabettech.com/i-asked-ai-to-count-my-carbs-27000-times)

### Inferences
- For Miqdar, the reliability of the scale connection is the diabetes feature that matters most to users. The top complaint category, connection and data access, maps to the phone-to-Pi link and to saving the diary.
- Miqdar's flow (weigh, photo, review) cuts typing, which answers the "tedious logging" complaint. Quick re-logging from Search also helps.
- Exporting the carb figures Miqdar shows, including 15 g portions, to Apple Health would avoid the Carb Manager complaint. Apple Health has a dietary carbohydrates type; portions would need converting back to grams.

### Gaps
- No systematic review of complaints specific to food or carb logging in diabetes apps was found. The JMIR study covers apps with device connections in general.
- No review mining of Arabic-language or Gulf users was found.
