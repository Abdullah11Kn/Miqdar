# Saudi and Gulf market for calorie-tracking apps, and what Saudi and Gulf users need

Researched 2026-11-14. Saudi Arabia first, then the GCC. Note on source quality: app features come mostly from vendor sites and app store pages, which are self-reported. Health figures come from GASTAT, IDF and peer-reviewed studies, but the GASTAT 2024 PDF returned 404 when fetched, so its figures are taken from search excerpts and from studies that cite it.

## 1. Which calorie and diet apps are used in Saudi Arabia and the GCC, what they offer, and what Arabic-speaking users complain about

### Takeaway
The market already has several Saudi-first trackers (Kilo, Loqma, Miran, Tabaq, Mezan) that offer Arabic, Saudi dish and restaurant-chain databases and AI photo or text logging, plus a large meal-plan delivery sector led by Calo. None of the ones checked mentions a kitchen scale, Ramadan mode or diabetes mode. The best user evidence (Riyadh, 2018 data) says people stop using weight apps because no specialist monitors them, because the app is not in their language, and because it is confusing.

### Cited Findings
**Local trackers (vendor claims, not verified)**
- Kilo (كيلو): Saudi-market iOS app. "Photo scan" reads a plate ingredient by ingredient with editable estimates; barcode scanner; 151,000+ foods and 4,000+ brands, including Saudi chains Albaik, Mama Noura and Kudu; restaurant items "verified" against published menus or KFC's GCC nutrition table; Arabic or English search; logging through ChatGPT and Claude linked to a Kilo account; reads steps from Apple Health; "Free to start". No mention of Ramadan, diabetes or a smart scale. — [Kilo](https://kiloappsa.com/)
- Loqma: AI tracker that estimates calories and macros from a meal description; describes itself as Saudi- and Gulf-first and says it is working on more accurate Saudi and Gulf portions. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/loqma-ai-calorie-tracker/id6779627300); [Loqma site](https://getloqma.com/en)
- Miran: built by Aqdar LTD, founded in Saudi Arabia in 2018; claims a 120,000+ food database and calorie data for restaurants such as Albaik and Kudu. — [Miran](https://miranapp.com/)
- Tabaq: claims "calories for real portions" and a 2M+ item database in English, Arabic and Turkish. — [Tabaq](https://tabaq.ai/en/)
- Mezan: described by a review site as one of the fastest-growing Arabic nutrition apps, with more than 1.9 million items (a marketing figure). — [Zorest blog](https://zorest.com/blog/best-arabic-calorie-counter-app)
- Global apps: a third-party comparison says MyFitnessPal is best for Arabic and Lebanese foods among global apps, and that Lose It!'s interface is English only. This is a blog, not a measurement. — [Mayobeid](https://mayobeid.com/en/blog/best-calorie-counting-apps/)
- An earlier academic Arabic weight-loss app, Akser Waznk (2019), used the King Abdulaziz Medical City diet manual and Saudi food composition tables for more than 100 foods. — [JMIR Formative Research 2019](https://formative.jmir.org/2019/1/e11785/PDF)

**Government apps**
- Sehhaty (MOH): the app's store listing names "Nutrition and Weight Management" and "Activity and Fitness" (steps, health challenges) among its services. — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.lean.sehhaty)
- The MOH website offers BMI, ideal body weight and calorie calculators. — [MOH Sehhaty support page](https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/support/pages/sehhatyapp.aspx)
- MOH ran public consultations on Sehhaty development (results published July 2025; a new consultation on enhancements in August 2026), framed under Vision 2030's Health Sector Transformation Program. — [MOH 2025-07-08](https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/ministry/mediacenter/ads/pages/ads-2025-07-08-001.aspx); [MOH 2026-08-15](https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/Ministry/MediaCenter/Ads/Pages/ads-2026-08-15-001.aspx)
- MOH launched school fitness screening through Sehhaty in August 2025. — [MOH news 2025-08-19](https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/ministry/mediacenter/news/pages/news-2025-08-19-001.aspx)
- A separate MOH-related app for hypertension, "Sehhaty Wa Daghty", is reported to include calorie counts for Saudi dishes; this is not the main Sehhaty app. — [JMIR Formative Research 2024](https://formative.jmir.org/2024/1/e60037/PDF)

**Meal-plan delivery**
- Calo (Riyadh): $25M Series B led by Nuwa Capital (December 2024, reported $250M valuation), then a $39M extension led by AlJazira Capital in July 2025, $64M Series B in total; over 10 million meals delivered in 2024; 50% year-on-year growth driven by Saudi Arabia, Kuwait and the UAE; plans a Saudi IPO targeted for 2027. — [Sharikat Mubasher](https://en.sharikatmubasher.com/news/article/21498282/saudi-foodtech-startup-calo-secures-39mn-in-series-b-extension-round?lang=en); [Startup Bahrain](https://startupbahrain.com/news/saudi-meal-subscription-startup-calo-raises-25-million-series-b-at-a-reported-valuation-of-250-million); [Enterprise AM](https://enterpriseam.com/ksa/2025/01/05/calo-closes-usd-25-mn-series-b-funding-eyes-tadawul-ipo/)
- Saudi Arabia was reported to be 70% of Calo's revenue and the UAE 15% at the time of its Series B. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2936815) (via search excerpt)

**What users say and need (survey evidence)**
- Riyadh web survey (Aljuraiban, data May to July 2018, published 2019): 43.07% (513/1191) had downloaded a weight-management app. 74.1% of users had stopped. Reasons: specialist monitoring not offered 26.58%, app not in the local language 24.47%, confusing to use 16.84%, hidden costs 15.26%, lost interest 12.37%. Among normal-weight users the top reason was language (31.3%); among overweight or obese users it was no specialist (33.9%), then language (20.3%). — [JMIR mHealth 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6406230/)
- Same survey, desired features: overweight or obese users wanted specialist monitoring 39.5%, barcode calorie identification 24.1%, nutrition information on many foods 17.6%, weekly or monthly progress reports 13.8%, reminders 5.0%. Normal-weight users: nutrition information on many foods 34.5%, barcode 27.8%, specialist 16.0%, reports 13.4%, reminders 8.3%. 33.14% downloaded an app to monitor food intake. — [JMIR mHealth 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6406230/)
- The same paper cites a 2015 qualitative study of Saudi women which found that available apps' diet programs "lacked locally available foods". — [JMIR mHealth 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6406230/)
- A 2020 quality evaluation of weight-management apps in Saudi Arabia found 30% (7/23) below the MARS acceptability score of 3.0, all of them Arabic-only, and said improvements to Arabic apps had been limited. — [JMIR mHealth 2020](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2020/10/e19844/) (figures from search excerpt; full text did not load)
- A 2025 survey of 403 non-healthcare users of Sehhaty found privacy and security (transparency, data protection) were major deterrents; cost barriers were minimal; font size, layout and lack of consistent internet were also reported. — [PMC 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12014757/)
- Egypt and Saudi cross-sectional study: 27.4% of 299 adults used mHealth apps; living in Saudi Arabia (vs Egypt) and physical activity were associated with higher use. The Saudi subsample was only 52. — [PMC 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9786142/)

### Inferences
- Arabic alone is no longer a differentiator: several Saudi apps have Arabic, Saudi dishes and Saudi chains. Miqdar's distinct points are measured grams from a real scale (no other app checked claims scale integration), home-cooked dishes rather than restaurant menus, and Ramadan and diabetes modes, which none of the checked local apps mentions.
- "Specialist monitoring not offered" was the top quit reason for overweight users. Miqdar's doctor's PDF report is a low-cost answer to this without accounts; it is worth showcasing.
- Barcode scanning was the second most wanted feature in 2018. Miqdar has none; packaged foods may be a gap, though the scale flow covers home food.
- Restaurant chain data (Albaik, Kudu) is something competitors lead on; Miqdar's away-from-scale logging relies on Gemini estimates instead.

### Gaps
- No usage share or download ranking for apps in Saudi Arabia was found (no data.ai or Sensor Tower figures in results). Whether MyFitnessPal or Fitbit is more used than local apps is unknown.
- Right Bite (UAE meal plans) and Tawakkalna's health features were not covered by any result.
- No collection of app store reviews from Arabic users was found; complaints about translation and RTL are inferred from the surveys, not from reviews.
- Kilo, Loqma, Miran, Tabaq and Mezan claims (database size, accuracy, "verified") are unverified.

## 2. Health context: obesity, diabetes and prediabetes; MOH and Vision 2030 nutrition programs; SFDA calorie labelling; Saudi food composition data

### Takeaway
Roughly a quarter of Saudi adults have diabetes by the IDF's modelled estimate and about a quarter have prediabetes by meta-analysis, while about one in four to five adults is obese. The SFDA has required calorie display on all menus since 2019, added salt, caffeine and calorie-burn labels from 1 July 2025, and in June 2026 published lab-analysed composition tables for 130 Saudi dishes, which is the most relevant data source for Miqdar.

### Cited Findings
**Obesity**
- GASTAT's National Health Survey 2024 (self-reported): adult obesity (15+) 23.1%, child obesity (2 to 14) 14.6%. — [GASTAT Health Determinants 2024](https://www.stats.gov.sa/documents/20117/2435259/Health+Determinants+Statistics+Publication+2024+EN.pdf) (from search excerpt; the PDF returned 404 when fetched)
- A 2020 national estimate using measured BMI put obesity at 24.7%, down from 35.6% (1995 to 2000) and 28.7% (2013). — [PMC 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7999834/)
- A 2024 systematic review found adult obesity ranging from 20% to 39% across studies. — [Saudi Pharmaceutical Journal 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1016/j.jsps.2024.102192)
- An MOH-hosted study reported 20.3% obesity and 38.7% overweight. — [MOH PDF](https://cdn-tr.moh.gov.sa/042025/638808256223852275_066931cf-a721-4b86-b0e5-0eea25f35ca0.pdf)

**Diabetes and prediabetes**
- IDF lists 23.1% adult diabetes prevalence for Saudi Arabia (modelled, includes undiagnosed). — [IDF Saudi Arabia](https://idf.org/our-network/regions-and-members/middle-east-and-north-africa/members/saudi-arabia/) (from search excerpt)
- Diagnosed diabetes is about 9.1% per GASTAT 2024, as cited by a 2026 Riyadh primary-care study, which itself found 12.4%. — [Frontiers in Clinical Diabetes and Healthcare 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/clinical-diabetes-and-healthcare/articles/10.3389/fcdhc.2026.1796490/pdf)
- Pooled national prediabetes prevalence among Saudi adults is 24.1% (95% CI 19.5% to 29.4%), from studies January 2000 to September 2024, markedly higher in men. — [PeerJ 2025 meta-analysis](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12374689/)
- GCC (IDF Atlas 11th edition): Qatar age-standardised 24.6%, about 46.4% undiagnosed, fifth highest worldwide (its estimates are extrapolated from Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Saudi Arabia and the UAE). — [IDF Diabetes Atlas, Qatar](https://diabetesatlas.org/data-by-location/country/qatar/)
- Kuwait 28.4% crude (25.6% age-standardised, 37.3% undiagnosed) and UAE 16.5% crude (20.7% age-standardised, 64% undiagnosed), per a regional news summary of the 2025 Atlas, not checked against IDF directly. — [menews247](https://menews247.com/?p=267753)
- Bahrain 9.0% and Oman 11.8% in the older 2021 (10th edition) factsheet. — [IDF MENA factsheet 2021](https://diabetesatlas.org/idfawp/resource-files/2021/11/IDF-Atlas-Factsheet-2021_MENA.pdf)

**Government programs**
- MOH weight guidance: reduce high-energy foods such as sugary drinks and fast food, decrease portion sizes and eat slowly. — [MOH Know Your Numbers](https://www.moh.gov.sa/eServices/Documents/know-your-numbers-en.pdf)
- Sehhaty enhancements are framed as aligning with Vision 2030's Health Sector Transformation Program. — [MOH 2026-08-15](https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/Ministry/MediaCenter/Ads/Pages/ads-2026-08-15-001.aspx)

**SFDA calorie labelling**
- Restaurants and cafes were required to display calories on menus by 31 December 2018. — [Arab News](https://arabnews.com/node/1352151/saudi-arabia); [Bird & Bird](https://twobirds.com/insights/2018/global/all-restaurants-in-saudi-arabia-to-display-ingredients-and-calories-by-31-december-2018)
- New rules from 1 July 2025: caffeine content disclosure, a "Salt" icon for items over 5 g salt (2,000 mg sodium), and the physical activity time needed to burn an item's calories. — [SFDA news 17639](https://www.sfda.gov.sa/en/news/17639); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-enforces-new-menu-rules-to-flag-salt-caffeine-and-calorie-burn-1.500183654)
- Scope includes print menus, menu boards, digital apps, and delivery websites and apps. — [Foodics](https://www.foodics.com/calorie-counts-on-menus-new-sfda-regulations-for-restaurants/) (vendor blog; details beyond this were not confirmed against SFDA text)

**Saudi food composition data**
- On 2026-06-25 SFDA's National Nutrition Committee released the "Saudi Food Composition Tables": 130 Saudi dishes, each prepared three times, at least 12 samples per dish, 49 macro- and micronutrients, over 19,000 analyses, free as a PDF. — [SFDA news 19089](https://www.sfda.gov.sa/en/news/19089); [SFCT PDF](https://www.sfda.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2026-04/SFCT-E.pdf)
- SFDA's national database also covers sodium in traditional and commercial Saudi foods. — [GCC Business News](https://www.gccbusinessnews.com/sfda-saudi-food-composition-tables/)
- A 2025 study analysed 25 traditional dishes from five regions (recipes from home cooks) and found many high in carbohydrates and saturated fat; it notes existing Saudi composition data are often outdated. — [Frontiers in Nutrition 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12641437/)

### Inferences
- Diabetes plus prediabetes covers close to half of Saudi adults by these estimates, so diabetes mode and carb-first display serve a large group, not a niche.
- With about 46% (Qatar) to 64% (UAE) of diabetes undiagnosed, the app could reasonably encourage screening, though it must not diagnose.
- The SFDA 2026 tables are a lab-analysed, free reference for 130 Saudi dishes. Miqdar's Gemini per-100 g values for kabsa, jareesh, saleeg and mandi could be checked against them as a sanity range, and used in an accuracy test. This does not contradict the "Gemini analyses every food" decision if used as a check only.
- Saudi users already see calories, salt icons and calorie-burn minutes on restaurant menus, so those concepts are familiar; a salt flag would match SFDA's 5 g rule.

### Gaps
- No official Vision 2030 or Health Sector Transformation obesity target number was found.
- The SFDA tables' per-dish values for kabsa and others were not read here (the PDF was not opened).
- GASTAT 2024 figures could not be read from the original PDF.

## 3. Cultural eating patterns that apps handle poorly

### Takeaway
Direct research on shared platters, eating by hand and household cooking in calorie apps was not found. The evidence that exists says Ramadan sharply changes intake and glucose patterns, traditional dishes are carb- and fat-heavy, and portion estimation is the weak point of dietary assessment; weighing is called the most accurate method.

### Cited Findings
- In 104 Saudi adults with diabetes, calorie and carbohydrate intake rose significantly during Ramadan in type 2 patients; fasting also had beneficial effects on weight, mainly in type 2. — [PubMed 38192134](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38192134/)
- A Fitbit pilot in 36 Saudis with type 2 diabetes found low activity and short sleep during Ramadan compared with after it. — [Paperity](https://paperity.org/p/244886780/impact-of-ramadan-on-physical-activity-and-sleeping-patterns-in-individuals-with-type-2)
- Continuous glucose monitoring in fasting adults with type 1 diabetes in Medina showed an exaggerated rise after iftar, sustained overnight, and a second rapid rise after suhoor. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7261298) (from search excerpt)
- The IDF-DAR "Ramadan Nutrition Plan" suggests 30 to 40% of calories at suhoor, 40 to 50% at iftar and 10 to 20% in optional snacks, and warns of late-fast hypoglycaemia and high glucose after sugary iftar desserts. — [Slideshare copy of the IDF RNP](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/c14-idf-the-ramadan-nutrition-plan-for-diabetes/73731340) (secondary copy)
- A review found that technology in Ramadan nutrition education can support glycaemic control and safe fasting. — [Thieme](https://thieme-connect.com/products/ejournals/pdf/10.1055/s-0044-1781437.pdf)
- Weighing food before and after eating is described as the most accurate intake method, but too burdensome for large surveys; recall methods are often inaccurate; photos and food models reduce estimation error. — [Frontiers in Nutrition 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnut.2024.1505606/full)
- Cultural and regional diversity complicates standardising portion sizes; a Saudi food atlas manual lists using traditional utensils and photographing portions as steps; the Arabic myfood24 database has portion photos for 30% of foods. — [Frontiers in Nutrition 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnut.2024.1505606/full); [Frontiers in Nutrition 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1658078/full)
- Traditional Saudi dishes recorded from home cooks are often high in carbohydrate and saturated fat. — [Frontiers in Nutrition 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12641437/)

### Inferences
- Miqdar's before-and-after weighing ("Weigh What's Left") is the method the literature calls most accurate; this is a strong, citable argument for the scale in the report.
- Ramadan's raised carb intake and the post-iftar and post-suhoor glucose rises support Miqdar's Ramadan mode and its carb-first diabetes display. The IDF split (iftar 40 to 50%, suhoor 30 to 40%) could inform a later per-meal guide, though Abdullah chose "no suhoor and iftar split" for now.
- Shared platters: a user eating from a family platter cannot weigh their own share on the scale. Weighing the platter before and after and dividing, or weighing a personal plate, are possible approaches; this is a reasoned guess, not from a source.
- Dates and Arabic coffee are small, frequent, often unlogged items; a quick "dates and coffee" re-log could help. No source was found on how often they are missed.

### Gaps
- No study was found on communal-platter or hand eating in dietary apps, on mothers or housekeepers cooking (the person who weighs may not be the eater), on weekend and gathering eating, or on dates and gahwa logging.
- The IDF RNP was read only through a secondary copy.

## 4. Studies of mHealth and diet-app use and acceptance in Saudi Arabia

### Takeaway
Saudis use health apps widely, but they abandon diet apps quickly; language, usability, lack of professional follow-up and privacy are the recurring barriers. The diet-specific evidence is old (2018 data).

### Cited Findings
- 43% of Riyadh respondents had a weight-management app and 74% of them stopped; language, confusion and hidden costs were leading reasons; respondents asked for Arabic and simpler food logging. — [JMIR mHealth 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6406230/)
- Arabic-only apps scored worst on quality (all 7 sub-threshold apps of 23 were Arabic-only). — [JMIR mHealth 2020](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2020/10/e19844/)
- Sehhaty barriers (n=403, 2025): privacy and data protection transparency, font size and layout, inconsistent internet; cost not a barrier. — [PMC 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12014757/)
- 27.4% mHealth use in an Egypt and Saudi sample; Saudi residence associated with higher use. — [PMC 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9786142/)

### Inferences
- "Confusing" and "simplify food logging" match Miqdar's one-button capture design; the scale removes manual portion entry, the hardest step.
- Hidden costs drove 15% of quits; a scale with no subscription is a plus to state openly.
- Font size and layout complaints in Sehhaty suggest checking Dynamic Type and large text in Arabic.

### Gaps
- No 2023 to 2026 Saudi survey specifically on calorie or diet apps was found. No studies on smart kitchen scales or AI photo recognition acceptance in Saudi Arabia were found.

## 5. Privacy expectations and the Saudi PDPL for health data in apps

### Takeaway
Under the PDPL, health data is sensitive: it needs explicit consent, minimisation and access limits, and transfers outside the Kingdom are restricted, with an SDAIA risk-assessment guideline (February 2025) covering continuous or large-scale sensitive transfers. Saudi users name privacy as a major deterrent. Miqdar's on-phone diary helps, but sending food photos to Google's Gemini is a transfer abroad that needs clear consent.

### Cited Findings
- Processing sensitive data, including health data, requires explicit consent and stricter procedures; legitimate interest cannot be used for sensitive data. — [Herbert Smith Freehills](https://www.hsf.com/insights/2023-11/saudi-arabias-personal-data-protection-law-–-what-you-need-to-know); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?c=SA&t=law)
- Health data requires extra controls such as access restrictions and minimisation. — [CMS health checklist](https://cms.law/en/media/law-now/files/data-protection-health-checklist)
- Article 29 restricts transfers outside Saudi Arabia; the Transfer Regulation requires a risk assessment where safeguards such as standard contractual clauses are used or sensitive data moves continuously or at large scale. SDAIA issued a Risk Assessment Guideline for transfers in February 2025. — [Clyde & Co 2025](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2025/03/update-on-saudi-arabia-risk-assessment-guidelines)
- Sources conflict on whether explicit consent alone is a valid transfer basis: a compliance blog lists consent as a mechanism; law-firm summaries describe narrower grounds plus SDAIA approval. — [SecurePrivacy blog](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/saudi-arabia-pdpl-2023-implementing-regulations-data-transfer-guide); contrasted with [Global Legal Post](https://www.globallegalpost.com/lawoverborders/data-protection-law-guide-1072382791/saudi-arabia-945755774)
- Privacy and data protection transparency were major deterrents to Sehhaty use. — [PMC 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12014757/)

### Inferences
- Keeping the diary on the phone, with no accounts, keeps most health data out of PDPL controller duties in practice, but this is not legal advice.
- Food photos plus weight and diabetes settings could be read as health-related. Gemini requests send photos, not the profile, to Google outside Saudi Arabia; Privacy & Data already says so, and an explicit consent step before the first Gemini request would fit the PDPL's explicit-consent rule. Diabetes status should not be sent in prompts.
- When Abdullah builds accounts and a backend, health data storage and transfer rules will apply directly; a Saudi or in-region host and a PDPL review would be needed.

### Gaps
- No source addressed whether food photos count as health data, or how PDPL applies to an on-device-only app or a student project. The consent-versus-SDAIA-approval question for transfers was not settled from primary text.
- Other GCC privacy laws (UAE PDPL, Qatar, Bahrain) were not covered.
